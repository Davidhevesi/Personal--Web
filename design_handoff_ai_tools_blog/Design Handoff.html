01

Visual Concept

Direction: The Research Desk

The blog should feel like a well-organized notebook kept by someone who reads everything. Informed, calm, lightly opinionated. Think long-form tech journalism crossed with a personal writing practice — not a SaaS product page.

The strongest visual reference is a warm printed page: generous margins, a measured type scale, plenty of breathing room. Color comes from restraint, not decoration. Hierarchy comes from type weight and spacing, not from cards and badges.

Feel like

Stratechery, The Diff, early Six Colors. A publication that trusts its readers. Clean, confident, slightly premium.

Not like

Futurism, AI tool landing pages, Product Hunt launches. No glass morphism. No gradient mesh heroes. No "Trusted by 10,000+ teams."

Three design principles

  1. Typography does the work. Headlines are set in a serif; body copy is comfortable at a long read. Most visual interest comes from type scale, not decoration.
  2. Whitespace earns trust. Generous spacing between sections signals confidence. Cramped, busy layouts signal desperation.
  3. Color is a tool, not a mood. Accent color is used sparingly — links, the verdict badge, the newsletter CTA. The rest is warm neutrals.

03

Typography

Lora (serif, Google Fonts) carries all headlines. It's bookish without being stiff — well-suited to long-form editorial. IBM Plex Sans handles body text and UI. It reads cleanly at small sizes and has a slightly technical, trustworthy tone appropriate for tool reviews. IBM Plex Mono appears only for metadata labels, tokens, and code.

Line length & rhythm rules

  • Article body text: maximum 680px wide (approximately 65–72 characters per line). Never full-bleed.
  • Body line-height: 1.72. H2 line-height: 1.3. Display: 1.15.
  • Paragraph spacing: 1em between paragraphs. No extra leading on short paragraphs.
  • Use text-wrap: pretty on body copy to eliminate single-word orphans at line ends.
  • Do not justify body text. Left-aligned only.

Layout rules

  • Page max-width: 1160px, centered with auto margins, 24px side padding on mobile.
  • Content column (articles): max-width: 680px, centered within the page.
  • TOC / sidebar: 260px fixed width, only visible on screens ≥1100px wide. Sticky within the article.
  • Section gap (homepage): 96px between top-level sections.
  • Component internal padding: 24px for cards; 16px for compact components.
  • Border-radius: 4px maximum everywhere. No pill shapes except category chips which use 2px. Avoid 12px+ radii — they soften the editorial feel.
  • Grid: 12-column with 24px gutters for large layouts; simpler single-column for article content.

Hero section

The hero is typographic only. No hero image, no background gradient. The site tagline runs large in Lora; the featured article appears directly beneath it as a large headline link with its category label and estimated read time.

  • Tagline: something short that states the site's purpose. Example: "Honest reviews of AI tools. Updated when it matters."
  • Featured article block: category label (mono, uppercase) → article title (serif, Display size) → one-sentence hook → read time.
  • A single border-bottom separates the hero from the article list below. No card, no box, no shadow.
  • Vertical padding: 80px top, 72px bottom.

Latest Articles list

Two-column on desktop, single-column on mobile. Each article row contains: category label (mono, 11px), title (serif, ~22px), one-line deck (sans, 14px, muted), and date + read time (mono, muted). No thumbnails. No card backgrounds. Articles are separated by a hairline border.

No thumbnail images on the homepage. The blog's value is the writing, not photography. Thumbnails invite clutter and force commitment to image production at scale. If there's strong demand later, revisit — but start without them.

Newsletter section

Full-width section with a --bg-subtle background, 96px vertical padding. Single column, centered. Headline in Lora (~30px), one-line description beneath, then email input + button on one row. The button uses --accent.

  • Input and button: same height (44px), same border radius, flush side-by-side on desktop. Stacked on mobile.
  • No social proof numbers unless they're real. "Join 4,000 readers" only if it's true — otherwise omit entirely.
  • Connect to ConvertKit, Beehiiv, or similar via a simple form action. No custom modal.

Article header

Category label (mono, uppercase, accent color) → H1 in Lora Display → updated date + read time in muted mono. No hero image. A single border-bottom below the header area. Vertical padding above: 56px. Do not display author name unless the site eventually has multiple contributors.

Quick Verdict box

The most important component on the post page. Appears just below the article header, before the TOC or body. It answers: "Should I read this whole thing, or do I just need the bottom line?"

  • Visual treatment: --bg-subtle background, left border in --accent (3px), 24px padding.
  • Content: 2–3 sentence verdict + a score or recommendation label (e.g. "Worth it for most users" or "Best for: power users on Claude API").
  • Sanity field: verdictSummary (text), verdictLabel (string), verdictScore (optional number 1–10).
  • Do not make this a complex widget. It should be readable in 10 seconds.

Table of Contents (TOC)

On desktop (≥1100px): sticky sidebar, 260px wide, positioned to the right of the body column using CSS Grid (680px + gap + 260px). Highlights the active H2 as the reader scrolls using IntersectionObserver. Active item gets --accent color. On mobile: collapsed, shown as a short dropdown above the article body.

Comparison table

Appears inline within the body when the article compares products. Uses a simple <table> with a sticky first column on mobile (for the row label). Header row gets --bg-subtle; alternating rows are not striped — use hairline borders only. Cells are left-aligned. On screens <680px, add overflow-x: auto to a wrapper div.

Pros / Cons

Two-column on desktop (side by side), single-column on mobile. Pros column has a faint green-accent left border; Cons has an amber-accent left border. Simple bulleted lists, no icons, no emoji. Title: "Pros" / "Cons" in H3 weight.

FAQ section

Accordion component. Each item: question in IBM Plex Sans 600 (16px), answer expands below. Animated with a simple CSS max-height transition. Add @type: FAQPage JSON-LD schema for SEO. Questions come from a Sanity faq array field (each item: question, answer as block content).

Next.js Developer Notes

Routing

  • / → Homepage (ISR, revalidate every 60s or on-demand via Sanity webhook)
  • /[slug] → Blog post (ISR, revalidate on publish)
  • /category/[slug] → Category page (ISR, revalidate on publish)

Data fetching

  • Use GROQ queries in lib/queries.ts. Never co-locate queries in page files.
  • Use Next.js App Router with React Server Components for all data fetching — no getStaticProps.
  • Sanity client: @sanity/client with a CDN-backed withConfig for production reads. Mutating requests (preview) use a token-backed client.
  • Enable Sanity Live Preview via @sanity/next-sanity for staging drafts.

SEO

  • Use Next.js generateMetadata() per page. Export alternates.canonical to prevent duplicate-content issues from pagination or tag pages.
  • Add Article JSON-LD schema on post pages: headline, dateModified, author.
  • Add FAQPage JSON-LD schema on posts that have a faq array.
  • Add WebSite + SearchAction schema on the homepage.
  • Use next/image for any images (ogImages, future thumbnails). Set sizes attribute properly.

Performance targets

  • Lighthouse score ≥ 95 on all pages. No layout shift from fonts (use font-display: swap + preload links).
  • Ship Google Fonts as self-hosted via next/font/google — this eliminates the external font request and ensures CLS = 0.
  • Do not load any client-side JS frameworks just for the newsletter form. A simple <form> action pointing to your email provider's endpoint is enough. Progressive-enhance with a small React component only if you need AJAX submission.
  • The TOC's IntersectionObserver is the only required client JS on the post page. Keep everything else server-rendered.

CSS approach

  • Use CSS custom properties (tokens) defined in a global :root block in globals.css. Match this spec's token names exactly for consistency.
  • Prefer CSS modules per-component for scoped styles, with global tokens always available via var(--token).
  • No Tailwind unless it's already in use. The design system here is thin enough that custom CSS is more maintainable at this scale.
  • The PortableTextRenderer component needs a companion .prose stylesheet that styles all block-content output (p, ul, ol, blockquote, h2, h3, strong, a, code). Write it explicitly — do not use Tailwind Typography plugin.
12

What to Avoid

These patterns appear by default in AI-generated frontends and make the blog look generic. Treat this as a checklist to audit the build before launch.

Visual anti-patterns

  • Purple-to-blue gradient backgrounds. The entire purple/indigo/AI-brand spectrum is overused. The accent is teal-green on warm cream. No gradients anywhere.
  • Cards nested inside cards. Article rows are not cards. The site has zero nested card patterns.
  • Rounded-square icon tiles above headings. Do not prefix section headings with a badge/chip/icon in a rounded box. If you need an icon, use a thin inline SVG in the copy, not a decorative container.
  • Metric blocks ("10,000+ users", "99% uptime"). This is a blog, not a SaaS product. Do not manufacture credibility metrics.
  • Hero images or stock photography. The blog starts image-free. No illustrated hero. No person-at-laptop stock photo.
  • Glass morphism / frosted surfaces. backdrop-filter: blur() is off-limits. It reads as dated AI-aesthetic immediately.
  • Bounce or elastic easing on any animation. Use standard ease-out or ease-in-out. Bounce easing signals "AI-generated" to experienced eyes.
  • Gray text on a colored background. If the background has color, use near-black text. Muted text only on neutral backgrounds.
  • Inter or system-ui as the heading font. Headings must be Lora. Inter for headings is the single most common tell of an AI-generated design in this category.
  • Pure black (#000000) or pure white (#ffffff). Always use the warm-toned token colors. Pure black feels harsh against warm backgrounds.
  • Excessive shadow on cards or elements. Box-shadow should be used sparingly — only on the TOC sticky sidebar (a faint drop) and modal overlays. Never a glowing shadow.
  • "Explore our features" or SaaS copy patterns. Every word of copy should sound like a person who has actually used the tools. No feature marketing language.

The fastest way to make this look generic: use Inter for headlines, wrap article rows in cards with border-radius: 12px, and add a purple gradient to the hero. Avoid all three and the design immediately reads as intentional.
