# Post Templates — davidhevesi.com

Four fill-in-the-blank templates so no post starts from a blank page. Each is built to (a) rank for its target keyword, (b) earn affiliate clicks, and (c) feed the newsletter. Replace `[bracketed]` text.

---

## TEMPLATE 1 — Verdict Review ("Is [Tool] Worth It?")

**Target keyword:** `is [tool] worth it` / `[tool] review`
**Goal:** rank the quick-win keyword, drive affiliate signup, capture email.

**Structure:**
1. **H1:** Is [Tool] Worth It in [Year]? An Honest Review
2. **The verdict, up front (2–3 sentences).** Who it's for, who should skip it, and your one-line call. *People searching "is it worth it" want the answer fast — give it, then justify.*
3. **What [Tool] costs** — exact pricing tiers, what's free vs paid.
4. **What I tested / how long I used it** — establishes the "independent, hands-on" trust signal.
5. **What it's great at** — 3–4 concrete strengths with real examples/screenshots.
6. **Where it falls short** — 2–3 honest weaknesses. *This is what makes the verdict credible — never skip it.*
7. **Who should pay for it / who shouldn't** — bullet two reader profiles.
8. **The bottom line** — restate verdict + affiliate link: *"[Try Tool free →]" / "[See current pricing →]"*
9. **Newsletter CTA:** "We test every new AI tool — get the verdict each Sunday. [Subscribe]"
10. **Internal links:** → the head-to-head comparison, → the relevant "Best X" roundup.

**Length:** 1,200–1,800 words. **Refresh:** quarterly (update price + verdict, change year in title).

## TEMPLATE 2 — Head-to-Head Comparison ("[A] vs [B]")

**Target keyword:** `[tool a] vs [tool b]`
**Goal:** capture high-intent "which should I buy" traffic; earn affiliate clicks on *both* tools.

**Structure:**
1. **H1:** [A] vs [B]: Which Should You Actually Pay For in [Year]?
2. **Quick answer box** — "Choose [A] if… / Choose [B] if…" in 2 bullets. *Win the featured snippet.*
3. **Comparison table** — Price · Best for · Standout feature · Biggest weakness · Free tier? (one row each).
4. **Round-by-round** — 4–5 head-to-head sections: Pricing, Output quality, Speed, Ease of use, [category-specific]. Declare a winner each round.
5. **Where each one wins** — the decisive use cases.
6. **The verdict** — your pick + the honest "but if you…" caveat. Affiliate links to both.
7. **Newsletter CTA** + **internal links** to both individual reviews and the parent roundup.

**Length:** 1,500–2,200 words. **Refresh:** quarterly or when either tool ships a major update.

## TEMPLATE 3 — New-Tool Radar (First Look)

**Target:** speed + opinion, not SEO. Newsletter + social fuel.
**Goal:** be first and have a take; convert curiosity into subscribers.

**Structure:**
1. **H1:** [Tool] Just Launched — Here's My First Take
2. **What it is, in one line.** What problem it claims to solve.
3. **What's actually new / interesting** — 2–3 bullets. Skip the press-release fluff.
4. **I tried it — here's what happened** — one concrete hands-on test + screenshot.
5. **Early verdict:** worth your attention now, worth watching, or skip? Be decisive.
6. **Does it change any of my existing verdicts?** — link to affected reviews/comparisons.
7. **Newsletter CTA:** "I cover every notable launch first — [subscribe] for the Sunday roundup."

**Length:** 500–900 words. **Speed > polish.** Publish within 24–48h of a launch.

## TEMPLATE 4 — Weekly Roundup ("This Week in AI Tools")

**Target:** the newsletter centerpiece + on-site archive.
**Goal:** the recurring promise that makes subscribing worth it; retention.

**Structure:**
1. **H1/Subject:** This Week in AI Tools — [Date]
2. **The one thing that mattered** — lead with the single biggest story + your take.
3. **3–5 quick hits** — launches, updates, price changes. One sentence + one opinion each. Link out.
4. **From the blog this week** — link your Mon review + Wed comparison.
5. **Tool of the week** — a pick (new or evergreen) with an affiliate link.
6. **One-line sign-off** + "Forward this to someone deciding which AI tool to pay for."

**Length:** scannable — 400–700 words. **Cadence:** every Sunday, no exceptions. Consistency *is* the product.

## Cross-cutting rules

- **Always lead with the verdict/answer.** Your readers are decision-stage, not browsing.
- **Honesty is the brand.** Every review needs real weaknesses; every comparison a real loser. That's why people trust (and link to) you.
- **Affiliate links: contextual, not spammy.** In the verdict and table, not jammed into every paragraph.
- **One consulting CTA — playbooks only.** "Want this set up for your team? [Get in touch]." Keep it off reviews so trust pages stay clean.
- **Every post captures email.** The Sunday roundup is the promise; the reviews are the reach.
- **Internal-link every post** into its cluster (review ↔ comparison ↔ roundup). Non-negotiable for a young site.
